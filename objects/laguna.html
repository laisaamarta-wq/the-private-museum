Turquoise that looks lit from inside, laid in a slow wave so the necklace still reads as water when nothing moves.

Why it is rareParaíba tourmaline was only discovered in the late 1980s. Its neon blue comes from copper, and fine stones are often called rarer than diamonds.

Copper is what makes a Paraíba glow. These stones were found in Mozambique and cut shallow, to let as much light out of them as possible.

Matching them took four years: thirty one stones of one exact blue, so the eye reads a single stream of water rather than a row of gems. The room was built around a pool so the necklace would always have its reflection.
